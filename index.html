What BRYC offers

From 8th grade to a career.

BRYC supports Fellows through high school, through college or job training, and into a career. Here's what families can expect at each step.

  1. 8th–10th grade

    Learn how to learn

    Fellows build self-regulated learning skills: how to plan, check their progress, and take charge of their own learning.

    • Small learning pods focused on a core class, each guided by a Learning Mentor
    • Habits that drive success: note-taking, time management, organization, and self-advocacy
    • Group work that builds confidence and friendships
  2. 10th–11th grade

    ACT prep

    ACT scores open doors to admission and scholarships, so ACT prep starts in 10th grade.

    • Small ACT classes tailored to each Fellow's strengths
    • Practice tests and online practice in small groups
    • BRYC covers most ACT registration and score report fees
    • Higher scores mean more scholarship money, including Louisiana's TOPS award
  3. 12th grade

    Plan your path

    Seniors choose their next step, and BRYC helps them get there.

    • One-on-one help with applications for college, training programs, and jobs
    • Financial aid help: filing the FAFSA early, finding schools with strong aid, and applying for scholarships
    • We sit down with families to compare financial aid offers
    • Career planning for Fellows heading straight into the workforce
  4. After high school

    Finish strong and launch a career

    Support doesn't stop at graduation. Fellows become Alumni and can count on BRYC for life.

    • A persistence team that helps Fellows through the first year, when students are most likely to leave school
    • Partnerships with colleges and universities across the country
    • Internships, scholarships, and networking that lead to career-track jobs

Every path after high school

Not every Fellow wants the same thing after graduation. We help each one choose a path that leads to a good career, then see it through.

  • Four-year university
  • Two-year college
  • Trade or technical program
  • Straight into a career

Support at every step

  • Mentors who know youLearning Mentors, college and career counselors, and volunteer mentors who learn your goals.
  • Check-ins that catch needs earlyProgram managers check in with Fellows regularly and make a plan when someone needs more support.
  • Tutoring and writing helpExtra help with classes, essays, and applications.
  • Mental health counselingSupport for the whole student, not just their grades.
  • Financial aid and scholarshipsHelp finding and winning money for school, plus scholarships just for Fellows.
  • TransportationBRYC provides transportation from most schools to our campuses.
  • Snacks and mealsFilling snacks every week, plus meals at special events like Welcome Week and Community Week.
  • Tours, retreats, and eventsCollege tours, retreats, and BRYC-wide events that build community.
How to apply

Four steps to apply.

Apply from a phone or a computer. Plan for about 30 minutes, with the student and a parent/guardian together.

    A parent/guardian signs up and adds the household's information once.

    Contact information, grade, school, and a recent report card or transcript.

  3. Answer three short questions

    In the student's own words. There are no right answers.

  4. Submit and track

    A parent/guardian adds their information and signs. Then follow the application's status in your portal.

          From your onboarding form

          Will your student ride BRYC transportation from school to campus?

          How it works

          • We pick Fellows up from participating schools and bring them to their assigned campus. We do not provide transportation home.
          • Routes depend on school location, enrollment, and capacity, and can change during the year.
          • We need at least five Fellows from the same school to consider a new route, so smaller schools may not always be served.
          • Once you sign up, we add your mobile number to BRYC’s texting line for pickup times, locations, changes, and delays.

          Transportation rules

          Boarding and pickup
          • Fellows must be at their pickup location on time. There is a 5-minute grace period for boarding.
          • Buses will not return once they leave.
          • Before your student’s first ride, we’ll text you their pickup time and location.
          • If a Fellow misses the bus, BRYC will not come back for them unless there is an emergency or mechanical issue.
          Behavior on the bus or van
          • Follow driver instructions right away.
          • Stay seated at all times, and keep all body parts inside the vehicle.
          • Use appropriate volume and language (no profanity or vulgar language).
          • Keep hands to yourself. No fighting.
          • No eating or drinking.
          • Van riders must wear seatbelts at all times.
          Communication and attendance
          • Pickup times, locations, schedule changes, delays, and emergencies are shared through BRYC’s texting line.
          • On early-dismissal days, BRYC monitors school calendars and lets families know ahead of time.
          • If your student won’t ride on a scheduled day, let BRYC know by text or call, or send a message through the texting line.
          • Attendance is taken every day.
          Discipline and absences
          • First offense: warning. Second offense: 2-week transportation suspension. Third offense: semester or full-year suspension, decided by the Director of Operations.
          • Three unexcused absences (no notice ahead of time) means a Fellow loses transportation. Reinstatement requires BRYC approval and a $30 non-refundable fee per semester.

          When are applications due?
          • 2026–2027 school year: applications are due January 22, 2027.
          • 2027–2028 school year: applications open February 1, 2027. They close April 30 for rising seniors and June 15 for all other grades.

          When applications are closed, you can still , and we'll let you know when they open.

          How does the application form work?

          Click and a parent/guardian creates a free BRYC household account to start the application. You don't need a Google account. Your progress saves as you go, and you can track your application's status in the portal.

          Please use student and guardian email addresses that you check regularly. That is how our team will contact you about your application.

          We strongly recommend reading all of the guidance on this page before you begin.

          Application components

          • Student contact, demographic, and school information
          • Student transcript or report card (document upload)
          • Highest ACT scores (document upload; rising seniors only)
          • Three short essay questions
          • Guardian contact information
          • Household information, including your annual household income range
          • Scheduling, transportation, and campus placement policies

          Note: BRYC uses academic information to prepare program materials for you. Your grades and scores will NOT impact our decision about your enrollment. If you're having trouble accessing your grades, ask your guidance counselor for help.

          Do I need to complete the application in one sitting?

          We encourage applicants to draft their responses to the short essay questions in a document before beginning the application.

          We recommend that applicants sit down with their guardian to complete the application. Please plan to spend at least 30 minutes. Your answers save automatically in your portal, so you can finish later if you need to. Documents must be attached before you submit.

          Do I need to take the ACT?

          Yes, if you are a rising senior. BRYC requests ACT score information from rising seniors to help us prepare postsecondary recommendations for you. If you are a rising senior and have NOT taken an official ACT, please contact admissions@thebryc.org for next steps.

          Note: We strongly encourage all rising seniors to take the school-based ACT in March of their junior year.

          Note: Rising 8th through 11th graders are not required to take an official ACT before applying.

          Do I need to reapply to BRYC every year?

          No. However, returning Fellows and guardians must complete BRYC's end-of-year survey in April or May to confirm enrollment for the coming school year.

          Remember: once you are in BRYC, you are held to a high standard. To remain in good standing, Fellows must attend at least 80% of BRYC programs and complete other grade-specific program expectations as directed by their program manager.

          What if I run into issues with the application form?

          Eligibility

          What are BRYC's eligibility requirements?

          Any student who will be in grades 8–12 in the school year they are applying for is eligible to apply for BRYC.

          BRYC prioritizes serving students from under-resourced families, and most BRYC families report an adjusted gross income of less than $75,000. However, students from families with higher household incomes are welcome and encouraged to apply and explain their interest in and "need for" BRYC in the application. Visit thebryc.org/eligibility (opens in a new tab) for an infographic illustrating what it means to "have a need for BRYC."

          To keep BRYC free for those with the greatest need, families reporting an adjusted gross income above $75,000 complete income verification, and a participation fee may be requested if income exceeds $110,000. BRYC looks at each family's full situation, not just income. See the Income Verification section for more information.

          Why have BRYC's eligibility requirements changed from previous years?

          In previous years, BRYC applicants were required to meet minimum GPA and ACT score requirements. Recognizing that these metrics could exclude students who would benefit from BRYC, we no longer use GPA or ACT scores to determine admission. We do continue to request this information when available in order to best prepare program materials for Fellows.

          BRYC is still a rigorous program with high expectations for attendance, conduct, and class participation. Regardless of current grades, applicants can look forward to being challenged and growing their understanding of the learning process.

          Income verification

          Why does BRYC need to know about my finances?

          BRYC prioritizes supporting the students and families most in need of our services. Before accepting new Fellows, we ask families to provide information that helps BRYC determine whether students come from financially advantaged households. We appreciate your cooperation!

          Note: All rising seniors must complete income verification, regardless of the income reported in the application, so that our counselors can prepare postsecondary recommendations.

          What is the income threshold for participating in BRYC?

          BRYC Fellows typically come from households with an adjusted gross income of $75,000 or less. However, students from families with household incomes above $75,000 are welcome and encouraged to apply. The application gives you an opportunity to explain why your student "has a need for BRYC."

          What if our household income is higher than the typical threshold?

          If a family reports an adjusted gross income (AGI) above $75,000, BRYC will ask for documentation to verify your income. The following documents are acceptable:

          • Preferred: Form 1040, 1040A, or 1040EZ
          • Documents proving you receive any of the following: unemployment, Social Security Disability, Social Security SSI, Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), or subsidized housing
          • If your child is enrolled in THRIVE Academy, we will accept their enrollment letter.
          • If your family does not qualify for government assistance and you have not filed taxes in the last three years, you may submit your most recent pay stub.

          BRYC reviews each family's full situation, not just income. If the household AGI is above $110,000, BRYC may request a participation fee.

          Do I need to provide financial information for all the adults in my household?

          Yes, but only if your family reports an adjusted gross income above $75,000. In that case, BRYC will request documentation for every guardian who lives in the household and whose income contributes to supporting the student.

          Why is there a fee for certain families? I thought BRYC was free!

          Since its founding in 2009, BRYC's programs have been free of charge, except for minimal fees for special activities like college tours. This reflects our mission to provide under-resourced students with the same high-quality college access programming that more affluent families can pay for.

          In BRYC's years of serving the Capital Region, we have learned that income is one of several factors affecting equitable access to higher education. While BRYC will remain focused on, and free for, lower-income families, we do not want to overlook students who face other disadvantages in the college admissions process. We consider applications from students of any income level, and families with an adjusted gross income above $75,000 complete income verification. Those above $110,000 may be asked to pay a participation fee. We consider each family's full situation, not just income. To keep BRYC accessible to the students who need it most, we limit the number of spots available to higher-income families.

          Guardians can email admissions@thebryc.org or contact the recruitment team to discuss any concerns about the participation fee or BRYC's other income policies.

          How does BRYC protect my sensitive financial information?

          We appreciate your cooperation with our process and uphold the highest standards of confidentiality. All documents are kept in a secure system that only authorized BRYC staff can access. Please do not mail original financial documents to BRYC.

          Locations

          Where is BRYC located?

          BRYC's after-school programs serve students at two locations:

          • BRYC Downtown: 460 N 11th Street, Baton Rouge, LA 70802
          • BRYC Airline: 14141 Airline Hwy, Suites A & B, Baton Rouge, LA 70817
          How do I know which location I will attend?

          All students are assigned to BRYC Downtown or BRYC Airline based on their school or home address. BRYC's aim in making these assignments is to ease the burden of transportation for students and families.

          Can I request a specific location?

          Yes, but we cannot guarantee that we will be able to grant your request. Location assignments are based on the student's home or school address and class sizes. We will make every effort to ensure location assignments are not a burden for students and families.

          What if a family has students in different grade levels?

          We will make every effort to assign all siblings to the same location.

          BRYC programming

          How much does BRYC cost?

          BRYC is free. We just ask for Fellows' and families' commitment. There are two exceptions. First, families who report an adjusted gross income above $75,000 complete income verification, and those above $110,000 may be asked to pay a participation fee. Second, there may be special activities, like college tours, when we ask families for a small fee to cover some expenses.

          When do I come to BRYC?

          Fellows come to BRYC one day per week (Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday). Your assigned program day depends on the school you attend. BRYC provides transportation from most schools to Fellows' assigned campus. Programs run from 4:30 to 7:15 pm, and homework and hangout time starts at 2:30. Guardians are responsible for picking up their Fellows at 7:15 pm.

          Note: You may request to change your program day if classroom space allows, but you may need to provide your own transportation to BRYC.

          How important is it for me to attend my mandatory weekly programs?

          To get the most out of BRYC, you need to be here. Fellows must attend at least 80% of their weekly mandatory programs to remain in good standing with BRYC. Fellows with lower than 80% attendance are at risk of being dismissed from BRYC.

          Can I attend BRYC virtually?

          No. BRYC has discontinued virtual programs.

          Does BRYC provide transportation?

          Yes! BRYC provides transportation by bus or van from most schools to our campuses on certain days of the week. Fellows board BRYC transportation at a designated time after school dismissal. At the beginning of each semester, guardians submit a form to reserve their Fellow's seat. Fellows who are licensed drivers may also park in BRYC's lot.

          Note: If you request to change your program day, you may need to provide your own transportation to BRYC.

          Note: BRYC does not provide transportation home after programs. Guardians must pick up Fellows at 7:15 pm.

          Can I be in BRYC and still participate in sports and other extracurricular activities?

          Absolutely! BRYC Fellows are involved in every extracurricular activity you can think of. If you have other activities or commitments, we just ask that you work with us to make sure you can balance those and BRYC.

          Will BRYC help me pay for college?

          BRYC covers most of a Fellow's fees for ACT registration, ACT score reports, and college and scholarship applications. We also provide Fellows with funds to help with their college transition. BRYC offers exclusive competitive scholarship opportunities for Fellows interested in healthcare, business, or legal fields. Otherwise, we help Fellows find and secure their own financial aid.

          If I'm in BRYC, am I guaranteed a scholarship?

          No. BRYC offers annual competitive scholarships for Fellows interested in healthcare, business, or legal fields. Otherwise, we help Fellows find and secure their own scholarships! The harder Fellows work, the more success they have getting scholarships.

          What happens to my BRYC status after I graduate high school?

          Once Fellows graduate high school, they become Alumni. BRYC's persistence team provides Alumni with resources to stay on track, graduate, and move on to their careers. Alumni can continue to count on BRYC for life.
